Please find below an update on the autocall validation following yesterday’s pre-MRF.

Summary
The autocall validation was presented at the pre-MRF on Thursday 12 November 2026, 14:00–15:00. After discussion of the validation’s scope, the OSS methodology and its accuracy, the forum provisionally concluded that RiskFlow’s autocall implementation is fit for its intended purpose. The validation will be presented for approval at the MRF next week, with the points raised at the pre-MRF addressed up front.

1. Pre-MRF presentation

The results and findings were presented within the context set out in previous updates:
each autocall is priced in RiskFlow as its booked components;
volatility is modelled as forward variance;
all trades are executed under the back-to-back framework.
The scope of the validation was stated explicitly in the document. Under the back-to-back framework, Investec carries no traded market risk, and official prices for P&L are sourced from the hedge counterparty. RiskFlow’s valuations are used to monitor counterparty credit exposure to the hedge counterparty, not for P&L. The validation assesses the suitability of RiskFlow for that purpose.
Two main points were discussed:
What MRF approval covers. The forum clarified that the approval sought is for the use of RiskFlow’s current autocall model to monitor counterparty credit exposure under the back-to-back framework. The forward-variance volatility model is not the preferred approach. However, it is adequate for this purpose, and we confirmed that we are comfortable with it on that basis.
The OSS Monte Carlo method (raised by Investec UK).
We explained that One-Step Survival is a variance reduction technique. It calculates the probability of the note surviving each observation date analytically and simulates only the surviving paths. As a result, it reaches a stable price with far fewer simulations than full-path Monte Carlo.
We demonstrated its accuracy using the convergence analysis and the base valuation and exposure comparisons against our independent full-path model, all of which agreed within the 1% tolerance.
Investec UK confirmed that they were comfortable with the OSS approach.
After these clarifications, Investec UK confirmed they were comfortable with the autocall implementation in the context in which it is used. The forum provisionally agreed that the model is fit for purpose.

2. MRF next week
To reflect the pre-MRF discussion, the MRF presentation will be structured as follows:

Context and approval sought: a summary of the pre-MRF discussion points, setting out the scope of the validation under the back-to-back framework and the specific approval requested.
OSS methodology: a brief explanation of the One-Step Survival method and its variance reduction benefit relative to the full-path Monte Carlo approach of our independent model.
Results and findings: a short summary of the base valuation, exposure and convergence results, and the key findings.
Conclusion: confirmation that we consider the model fit for purpose for counterparty credit exposure monitoring, with the recommendation to move to a more sophisticated volatility model, stochastic or local-stochastic volatility, in future.
