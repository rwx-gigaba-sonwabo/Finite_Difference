7.1 Pricing approach

RiskFlow does not price the Autocall Note as a single instrument. Consistent with the replication structure set out in Section 3, the note is split into its component legs and each leg is priced on its own basis:

The Autocall Swap (ACS) — the coupon and conditional par-redemption leg, plus the floating funding leg carried via the IRS — is priced by QEDI_CustomAutocallSwap using a Monte Carlo simulation of the underlying under a flat (constant) ATM volatility, with the barrier date left unset. With no barrier date set, this instrument prices only the autocall coupon and floating payments; it carries no put and runs with SpotModel="None" (i.e. no stochastic/local-vol spot process is attached — the simulation uses the instrument’s own flat-vol GBM diffusion rather than a calibrated spot model).
The put leg of the note — the terminal downside the investor is exposed to below the 70% threshold — is not booked inside the ACS at all. It is replicated, as described in Section 3, through two long/short pairs of vanilla and digital options and their knock-in contras (① Vanilla put, ② Digital put, ③ Up-and-in put, ④ Up-and-in digital put). ① and ② are priced by closed-form solution (Section 7.4); ③ and ④, being barrier options, are priced by a second flat-vol GBM Monte Carlo, run at a different flat volatility to ① and ②’s closed form inputs (Section 7.3).

So “two flat-volatility GBM Monte Carlo models” in the original draft refers to two separate simulations — one inside the ACS (coupon/floating), one inside the up-and-in barrier legs (③/④) — each using its own flat vol input, not two competing models of the same leg.

7.2 Mapping from FA to Riskflow

Front Arena has no native autocall booking structure, so the note as booked in FA is not a single trade. It is represented as the combination set out in Section 3: one ACS (coupon leg + survival-conditional floating), one IRS (the non-cancellable funding leg, zero weight, populating the Autocall Floating via QEDI_UpdateAutocallCoupons), and the four option legs (①–④) booked as explicit vanilla/digital and up-and-in vanilla/digital trades with their contras, netted against each other.

RiskFlow’s mapping mirrors this one-for-one: each FA trade maps to its own RiskFlow instrument and is priced independently (ACS via QEDI_CustomAutocallSwap, IRS via the standard swap pricer, ①/② via closed-form, ③/④ via barrier Monte Carlo), and the validation re-aggregates them exactly as FA nets them — Payoff = Par + ACS + ① + ② + ③ + ④ (Section 3.9) — rather than attempting to value the note as one combined RiskFlow instrument. This is why the QEDI_CustomAutocallSwap caveat in 7.1 matters for the mapping: the ACS instrument is deliberately only ever used for the coupon/floating piece of the replication, and the put economics are picked up entirely by the other four legs. A mapping that assumed QEDI_CustomAutocallSwap priced the whole note would double the put exposure (once inside a “full” autocall instrument, once again in the four option legs) or miss it entirely if the barrier date were left unset without the option legs being booked alongside it.

7.3 Two volatilities for the Flat Volatility GBM models

Both Monte Carlo legs use a flat vol, but a flat vol applied at a different point on the smile, and in two different senses of “flat”:

ACS (coupon/floating) — forward (piecewise ATM) flat vol. The simulation needs a single number to drive the GBM diffusion for each step of the note’s life, but the note’s ATM-implied vol genuinely differs by maturity (the term structure is not flat). RiskFlow resolves this with a piecewise-constant ATM forward vol: for a path step falling between two adjacent autocall/coupon observation dates $t_i$ and $t_{i+1}$, the diffusion uses the forward volatility implied between those two tenors — i.e. the volatility consistent with the ATM term variance observed to $t_{i+1}$ net of the ATM term variance already observed to $t_i$:

$$\sigma_{fwd}(t_i, t_{i+1}) = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{ATM}(t_{i+1})^2 \cdot t_{i+1} - \sigma_{ATM}(t_i)^2 \cdot t_i}{t_{i+1} - t_i}}$$

This is the standard “strip the term structure into forward-starting variance” construction: $\sigma_{ATM}(t_i)$ and $\sigma_{ATM}(t_{i+1})$ are each flat (constant-to-date) ATM vols read directly off the ATM curve, but the number actually fed to the simulator over $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$ is the incremental vol consistent with both endpoints, not either ATM vol itself. Walking through the note’s autocall/coupon schedule this way — updating to the next bucket’s forward vol at each observation date — means the simulation reprices the correct cumulative ATM variance to every observation date along the path, even though within any single step the vol used is constant. This is the “piecewise method” referred to in the earlier draft: each step uses one ATM-anchored forward vol, held flat until the next observation date, then rolled to the next.

③/④ (up-and-in options) — single flat vol at the lower (70%) strike. These two barrier legs are not stepping through a coupon schedule in the same way the ACS’s path-dependent payoff does — they need one consistent vol input for the barrier Monte Carlo over the option’s own life, so RiskFlow uses the single flat implied vol read off the smile at the 70% strike (the vanilla/digital put strike, not ATM) at the option’s own maturity. This is the vol level actually relevant to the leg being priced: ③/④ exist specifically to cancel out ①/② once the note autocalls, and ①/② are themselves struck and valued at 70% (Section 7.4), so using the 70%-strike vol for the barrier legs keeps ③/④ consistent with the closed-form legs they are designed to offset.

7.4 Closed Form solution for the vanilla put and digital option

The vanilla put (①) and digital put (②) are both European-style with no path dependency, so RiskFlow prices them with closed-form solutions rather than Monte Carlo — standard Black-Scholes for ①, and the standard closed-form digital/binary put formula for ②, both evaluated at the 70% strike using the flat implied vol read off the smile at that strike and the option’s maturity (the same vol level described above for ③/④, since ①/② and ③/④ are priced consistently with one another by construction).

8. Variations that differ from the approach given above

It is assumed for this validation all trades are booked using the exact methodology given above — i.e. using the same structure of one ACS, one vanilla put, one digital put, one contra vanilla put (up-and-in), one contra digital put (up-and-in digital), and one IRS, all with the weighting structure described above, and with the ACS always booked without a barrier date so that it prices coupon/floating only and the put economics are carried entirely by the four option legs. Any autocall booked using a different replication approach — for example, a barrier date set directly on the ACS, a different spot model attached to the ACS, or the put leg represented by fewer or different option legs than ①–④ — will not be included in this validation, since the vol-usage mapping in 7.1–7.3 above (forward piecewise ATM vol for the ACS, flat 70%-strike vol for the barrier/closed-form legs) is specific to this exact booking structure and would not carry over unchanged to a different one.
